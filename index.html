Journal Publications

Abstract: This study aims to test the market anomaly of herding behavior driven by the response to supply chain disruptions in extreme market conditions such as those observed during COVID-19. The authors empirically test the response of the capital market participants for B2B firms, resulting in herding behavior. Using the event study approach based on the market model, the authors test the impact of supply chain disruptions and resultant herding behavior across six sectors and among different B2B firms. The authors used cumulative average abnormal returns (CAAR) and cross-sectional absolute deviation (CSAD) to examine the significance of herding behavior across sectors. The event study results show a significant effect of COVID-19 due to supply chain disruptions across specific sectors. Herding was detected across the automotive and pharmaceutical sectors.

Khan, A., Sindhwani, R., Atif, M. and Varma, A. (2024), "Supply chain driven herding behavior during COVID-19: evidence of interdependence from India", Journal of Business & Industrial Marketing, Vol. 39 No. 8 pp. 1764-1787.

Abstract: The extant literature establishes accounting information system (AIS) quality and dynamic-accounting analytic system capabilities as antecedents of superior firm performance. Yet the mechanism through which the two interact with firms' embedded management control systems (MCS) is partially understood at best. Moreover, research has not addressed whether this mechanism is expected to be linear or non-linear and whether an optimum level of accounting analytic capabilities exists. This study clarifies the linkage between AIS quality, dynamic accounting analytic system capabilities, MCS, and firm performance by drawing upon Simons' levers of control framework.

Varma, A., Sharma, A., & Khan, A. (2025). Accounting-analytic capabilities’ impact on management controls and performance. Journal of Computer Information Systems, 1-16.
https://doi.org/10.1080/08874417.2024.2441770 (ABDC=A, ABS=2, Scopus indexed)

Abstract: Controls are used to ensure "that resources are obtained and used efficiently and effectively to achieve the organization's objective" (Merchant and Otley, 2007, p. 788). Hybrid workplaces, which include flexible work arrangements like working from home, working outside of regular business hours, having flexible work hours, etc., are characterized by spatial and/or temporal separation. Through a quasi-experiment utilizing the theoretical underpinning of Simon's levers of control (1995, 2000), this study explores the types of controls which enable hybrid firm's competitiveness. The results of the experiment identify that the best possible combination to enable hybrid firm competitiveness is 'High IC and High DC'.

Varma, A. & Khan, A. (2023). Simons levers of control disentangled: A quasi-experiment into the competitiveness of hybrid firms. Management Control: 2, 2023, 19-41.
https://doi.org/10.3280/MACO2023-002002 (ABDC=C)

Research Pipeline

Currently working on M&A for thesis and intend to continue exploring and deepening research in this field over the long term. By leveraging large-scale financial datasets from Bloomberg and applying rigorous econometric techniques in R, thesis aims to make a meaningful and sustained contribution to the M&A and corporate finance literature. Exploring how the target firm's overall complexity influences the acquiring firm's post-acquisition short-term, medium-term, and long-term performance, integration, risks, and deal duration.

  • Exploring the role of management controls during post-acquisition integration
  • Employee and top management perceptions about management control during cultural differences arise due to M&A deals/transactions
  • Predicting M&A deal success using an econometrics model and financial indicators, and how does corporate governance shape the M&A outcomes?
